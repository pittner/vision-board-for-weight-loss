12 honest questions before you choose your goals

Answer them alone, with a notebook, in 15 minutes. Be honest, nobody else reads it. The answers that surprise you point to the goals that will actually stick.

  1. Why do I want to lose weight, really, beyond how it looks?
  2. What has made past attempts stop, and when exactly did they stop?
  3. When do I eat without being hungry, and what is happening then?
  4. What healthy food do I actually enjoy, not just tolerate?
  5. What kind of movement have I ever kept up for more than a month?
  6. What does a realistic good day of eating look like for me?
  7. Who can support me, and who quietly makes it harder?
  8. What will I tell myself on the day the scale does not move?
  9. How much sleep am I really getting, and how does it change my cravings?
  10. What is one thing I can do for 10 minutes even on my worst day?
  11. What will I do differently the next time I slip?
  12. If this year goes really well, what is different about my body and energy by next winter?

Plan your weight loss year in 45 minutes

  1. 10 minutes: look back honestly. Write what stopped your past attempts and the moments you eat without hunger. The patterns point to your plan.
  2. 15 minutes: answer the questions. Go through the 12 questions below. Short, honest answers are enough; nobody else reads them.
  3. 10 minutes: pick three goals. Not ten. One for eating, one for movement, one for the habit or mindset that keeps tripping you up.
  4. 10 minutes: make it visible. Turn your most important goal and its first step into a phone lock screen, so you see it every time you reach for a snack.

Questions

What should go on a weight loss vision board?

Put the goal in your own words, a first step you can do this week, and a reminder of your real why. This page has 36 weight loss goals in 6 groups (eating, movement, habits, mindset, sleep, tracking), each with a first step, so you can pick three and make them visible.

Does a vision board actually help with weight loss?

A board does not burn calories, but seeing your goal and your next step every day keeps the decision in front of you. The habit that works best is putting it on your phone lock screen, which you look at dozens of times a day, right when cravings hit.

How many goals should I put on it?

Three is enough: one about what you eat, one about moving more, and one about the habit or feeling that keeps derailing you. Fewer goals get more attention and stick longer.

What are realistic weight loss goals for 2027?

Small and specific beats big and vague: walk 8,000 steps a day, eat protein with every meal, cook at home five days a week, go to bed before 11 pm. Aim for the next 1 kg, not the final 20, and measure more than the scale.
